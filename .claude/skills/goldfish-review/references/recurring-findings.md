# Recurring findings (pre-empt these while drafting)

These are the classes of error a fresh-context review keeps finding in drafts that already looked finished. Examples use an imaginary vendor so they apply to any subject.

1. **A source's claim stated as fact.** "The sensor passed the heat test" when it is the vendor's own statement. Attribute it ("the vendor says") and keep the source's qualifier ("at 40 degrees, for one hour").
2. **An inference presented as sourced.** "Customers were not told" when the source only says it does not mention notification. Say what the source says, or label the sentence as your own view.
3. **Text and image disagree.** The text says one thing and a chart label or slide says another, or the text says "no recall" while the image says "no recall announced". Use the source's exact words in both and keep the unknown narrow.
4. **A dropped condition.** The source says a rule applies "where a product has a serial number"; the draft states it as universal. Keep the condition or cut the item.
5. **A claim of absence made without searching.** "The announcement names no supplier." Search the source for the word first.
6. **A paraphrase stronger than the text.** "Fixed" for "mitigated", "minor" for "unlikely to have appreciable effects", "must" for "may", "the build" for "the changes".
7. **A recommendation with no source.** A checklist item or "what to do" chip that nothing supports. Tie it to a source clause, or label it as your own view.
8. **A reported fact loses its reporter.** "Delayed until spring" is one outlet's wording. Attribute reported items to the outlet in both the text and the image.
9. **A falsifier that tests a position nobody holds.** "I would change my mind if re-testing every unit showed better results" when nobody proposes that. The line must test the text's own recommendation ("if teams without such a log showed equally good outcomes"). It is the author's opinion line: propose the change, do not make it silently.
10. **Opinion in the same voice as evidence.** Mark opinion with "My view:".
11. **A constructed number on a chart.** A bar or count with no source. Name what it counts, or drop it.
12. **Definitions that overreach.** A term defined in the draft's own words instead of the source's words.
